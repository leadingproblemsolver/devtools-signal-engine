# Dalus — 5-account ICP work sample

Date: 2026-08-17

## Purpose

A small, evidence-bounded prospecting sample for Dalus. The goal is not to claim buying intent. It is to identify hardware organizations whose public engineering work exhibits the exact complexity Dalus is built around: system architecture, requirements, traceability, cross-functional integration, verification, and preservation of engineering knowledge.

Dalus public positioning used for qualification: AI-native MBSE for complex hardware teams in aerospace, defense, energy, automotive, and robotics; collaborative system architecture, requirements management, simulation, and knowledge preservation.

## Qualification rule

An account enters the set only when public evidence shows at least three of the following:

1. Complex multi-subsystem hardware.
2. Explicit system architecture / systems engineering ownership.
3. Requirements decomposition or traceability.
4. Verification, validation, test, or safety-critical workflows.
5. Cross-functional hardware/software integration.
6. High cost of late design change, inconsistency, or undocumented knowledge.

Scores represent **observable Dalus relevance**, not purchase probability.

## 1. Shield AI — 5/5

**Observed**

- Building autonomous aircraft/UAS spanning avionics, autonomy, propulsion, sensors, mission systems, manufacturing, and flight operations.
- Current systems role explicitly requires system architecture, requirements decomposition, lifecycle traceability, MBSE, SysML/DOORS, trade studies, safety analyses, and flight-test support.
- The role also calls for modes/states/transitions and fault responses across mission-critical systems.

**Why this maps to Dalus**

This is almost a direct expression of the Dalus problem surface: a changing, multidisciplinary system whose requirements, architecture, interfaces, behavior, safety evidence, and verification state must remain coherent.

**Likely owner**

Director / VP Systems Engineering; Chief Engineer; Systems Architecture lead.

**Unknown**

- Existing MBSE stack maturity.
- Whether procurement/security constraints permit a new cloud platform.
- Whether the need is acute enough to displace existing Cameo/DOORS workflows.

**Next GTM action**

Lead with a migration/coexistence question rather than a generic demo: where does model/requirements truth currently diverge between autonomy, avionics, test, and safety artifacts?

## 2. Astranis — 5/5

**Observed**

- Designs, builds, and operates satellites in-house.
- Current Spacecraft Engineer role owns complete spacecraft system architecture, cross-system trade studies, mission CONOPs, unit/vehicle test planning, subsystem interfaces, mass/power budgets, and anomaly resolution.
- Role references Smartsheet, Jira, and Confluence, suggesting system knowledge is distributed across multiple operational surfaces.

**Why this maps to Dalus**

The account has a strong single-source-of-truth problem surface: architecture + interfaces + requirements + budgets + test readiness + anomalies across a full spacecraft lifecycle.

**Likely owner**

Spacecraft Systems lead; Chief Engineer; VP Engineering; technical program leadership.

**Unknown**

- Current formal MBSE tooling.
- Degree of internal tooling already replacing legacy systems engineering software.
- Security/export constraints around cloud-hosted engineering data.

**Next GTM action**

Test whether maintaining architecture/interface truth across spacecraft revisions is still document/tool fragmented, and whether versioned system models would reduce reconciliation work.

## 3. Radiant Industries — 4.5/5

**Observed**

- Developing the Kaleidos microreactor and preparing a nuclear demonstration/test campaign.
- Current nuclear engineering work collects testing requirements, objectives, and success criteria; develops procedures constrained by system operating bounds; performs analyses to verify the testing sequence; and coordinates with systems/operations engineers.
- Public roles also reference hardware-system requirements, BOM ownership, supplier development, subassembly design/manufacturing/test schedules, and cross-functional requirement collection.

**Why this maps to Dalus**

Safety-critical nuclear hardware creates high cost for requirement ambiguity and broken traceability between design intent, analysis, procedures, verification criteria, and operational constraints.

**Likely owner**

Systems Engineering lead; Chief Engineer; test/verification program lead.

**Unknown**

- Whether their compliance workflow mandates incumbent tooling.
- Whether Dalus currently satisfies nuclear-specific assurance/document controls.
- Current systems-modeling maturity.

**Next GTM action**

Probe one narrow workflow: requirement → analysis → test success criterion → written procedure traceability during design changes.

## 4. Machina Labs — 4/5

**Observed**

- Builds industrial robotic manufacturing systems with tooling, sensors, closed-loop control, CAD/CAM, robotics, automation, and production deployment.
- Current roles require cross-disciplinary requirements definition, validation of hardware/software releases, production-cell testing, issue triage, detailed records of process changes, and customer-to-engineering feedback loops.
- Forward-deployed work explicitly bridges customer requirements with Product, Software, Manufacturing Engineering, and Robotic Process Engineering.

**Why this maps to Dalus**

Strong hardware/software integration and validation complexity. The fit is less directly MBSE-explicit than Shield AI/Astranis, but the coordination and traceability burden is observable.

**Likely owner**

VP Engineering; Robotics Systems/Automation lead; technical program lead.

**Unknown**

- Whether system architecture is sufficiently complex to justify formal MBSE.
- Whether their internal software already centralizes most engineering truth.

**Next GTM action**

Validate whether customer-specific cell configurations create recurring requirements/interface/version reconciliation work across mechanical, robotics, controls, and software teams.

## 5. Hadrian — 3.5/5

**Observed**

- Scaling automated aerospace/defense manufacturing across multiple factories and new capabilities.
- Current roles emphasize controlled documentation, quality requirements, software-supported manufacturing systems, customer technical expectations, process integrity, and cross-functional engineering/operations coordination.
- A current Workforce Systems role describes building a capability operating system with controlled documentation and structural integrity as the company scales.

**Why this maps to Dalus**

The organization clearly has configuration/documentation/requirements complexity, but the public evidence is more manufacturing-process-centric than system-design-centric. This is a deliberate lower-confidence inclusion.

**Likely owner**

Systems/Quality Engineering; technical program leadership; engineering operations.

**Unknown**

- Whether Hadrian itself designs sufficiently complex end systems versus primarily manufacturing parts/systems for customers.
- Whether MBSE is an active workflow rather than adjacent process infrastructure.

**Next GTM action**

Do not pitch yet. First determine whether Hadrian owns system-level product architecture on its newer complete-system programs; reject the account if the answer is no.

## False-positive discipline

The largest likely false positive is Hadrian. Public evidence demonstrates manufacturing and documentation complexity, but not enough evidence yet proves that Dalus-style system modeling is a priority workflow. It should be validated before outbound.

## What I would test next

Rather than adding 100 more companies, I would manually validate 5–10 accounts like these against one question:

> Where does system truth become expensive to reconcile when a hardware design changes?

I would then classify the answer into: requirements, interfaces, architecture, verification/test, knowledge loss, or version/configuration management—and use that to refine the ICP and message rather than treating all complex hardware companies as equivalent.
